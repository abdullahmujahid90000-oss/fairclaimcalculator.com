How to Use Each Letter Mode

Each mode is meant for a specific, narrow ask — not a general complaint. A valuation report request is usually the right first step any time you've only been given a summary or a verbal number: you're entitled to see the actual report before you can meaningfully evaluate it. A factual correction request only makes sense once you've actually reviewed that report and found something concrete and specific — a wrong trim, wrong mileage, a missing option package — not a general feeling that the number is too low. A comparable-vehicle reconsideration request works well after you've gathered your own comps (the Total-Loss Offer Audit tool helps organize these). A diminished-value claim notice is for repaired vehicles, not total losses, and should only reference facts you can support. A request for written explanation of adjustment is useful when a specific line item — like a "typical negotiation" deduction — isn't explained anywhere in the report. And a request for policy appraisal-clause language is a good first step before considering the formal appraisal process, since that clause's exact terms vary policy to policy.

Example: After running the Total-Loss Offer Audit, an owner finds the insurer's report shows a comparable listing that's actually a stale, already-sold listing three counties away. Rather than writing a general complaint, they use the Comparable-Vehicle Reconsideration Request mode, describe the specific comps they found instead — three active listings within 25 miles, same trim and mileage band — and ask, in writing, for a response.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will this letter guarantee a better offer?

No. It's a factual, calm request or notice — not a legal filing and not a negotiation tactic. It does not include threats, fake deadlines, or legal citations, and it doesn't guarantee any particular response or outcome.

Do I need to fill in every field?

No. Any field you leave blank is replaced with a bracketed placeholder like [Insurer Name] so you can fill it in yourself before sending. The letter is fully editable in the text box before you copy, print, or save it.

Does this send the letter for me or store what I write?

No. Nothing you type is sent to a server. The letter is generated and edited entirely in your browser, and it's your responsibility to review, personalize, and send it yourself.
